Spirit: The Rover That Got Stuck But Never Gave Up

Spirit (also called MER-A) was a NASA Mars rover and the twin of Opportunity. Launched on June 10, 2003, it landed in Gusev Crater on January 4, 2004, to search for evidence of ancient water.

Spirit found rocks altered by water, pure silica proving ancient hot water, and carbonate minerals—a crucial discovery showing Mars was once warm, wet, and potentially habitable. Across its journey, Spirit traveled over 7.73 km (4.8 miles) and explored the Columbia Hills.

Originally designed to last for only 90 days, Spirit exceeded all expectations by operating for more than 6 years (2,208 sols)!

In 2009, Spirit got stuck in soft sand at a place called Troy. After losing solar power during the Martian winter, it sent its final signal on March 22, 2010, permanently remaining a station on Mars.
