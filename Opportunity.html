Opportunity Rover

The Opportunity Rover Mission

Opportunity launched with its twin Spirit, both landed in 2004 to find water. It landed on 25 January 2004 at 05:05 UTC in Eagle Crater inside Meridiani Planum. It landed inside the crater by luck, never happened before. It was supposed to work 90 days, but worked 15 years. First thing it found in Eagle Crater was rocks with Hematite, a mineral that forms only in water. That was first evidence Mars had water. Then it went to Endurance Crater, stayed from June to December 2004, studied layered rocks. On the way it found an iron-nickel meteorite called Heat Shield Rock near its heat shield. In 2005 it got stuck in sand for 6 weeks, NASA moved it centimeter by centimeter. Then it went to Victoria Crater, 800 meters diameter, arrived after 21 months travel in September 2006, stayed inside for a year. In 2007 a big dust storm, power dropped to 128 watt hours. Then it went to Endeavour Crater, huge crater 22 km diameter, arrived in 2011. Kept driving south along its western rim. On 10 June 2018, a global dust storm covered Mars, Sun disappeared, solar panels could not charge, battery died. Last message was: My battery is low and it's getting dark. NASA sent more than 1000 signals from August 2018 to February 2019, no response. On 13 February 2019 NASA declared Opportunity dead officially.

It was an Opportunity to uncover the mysteries of Mars to search if life on Mars has ever existed, even at early stages. The Opportunity Rover gave us more than 217,000 pictures.

But it couldn't prove that life ever existed on Mars; it only collected clues suggesting that Mars once had liquid water and other conditions that might have been suitable for life to exist.

Also, it was originally designed to last for 90 Martian days (92.5 Earth days), but it exceeded it all by 14 years, 47 days. Which was a scientific marvel!

  4. Curiosity: 38.15 km, still driving on Mars
  5. Apollo 17 Lunar Rover: 35.74 km, 1972, on the Moon
  6. Apollo 15 Lunar Rover: 27.8 km, 1971, on the Moon
  7. Apollo 16 Lunar Rover: 27.1 km, 1972, on the Moon
  8. Lunokhod 1 (USSR): 10.5 km, 1970-1971, on the Moon
