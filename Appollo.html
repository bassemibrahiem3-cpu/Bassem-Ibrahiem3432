Apollo Lunar Rover

It was an Opportunity—or rather, a monumental leap—for humanity to drive the first car on another world: the Apollo Lunar Rover (LRV).

Built by Boeing and General Motors, it was used on only three missions: Apollo 15, 16, and 17 (in 1971 and 1972). Before the LRV, astronauts could only walk about 500 meters from the lander. With the rover, they could drive up to 8 km away!

It was a battery-powered marvel with no steering wheel (controlled by a joystick), special wire-mesh wheels, and reached a top speed of 18 km/h (11.2 mph) driven by Eugene Cernan on Apollo 17—the fastest speed ever driven on the Moon!

Together, the three rovers covered a total distance of 90.64 km across the lunar surface. When the missions ended, they were left behind, and 3 LRVs remain on the Moon today to stay there forever.

1969

NASA decided they needed a car for the Moon. Boeing won the contract to build it.

May 1969

Boeing started to build the LRV, with General Motors.

May 1971

The first LRV was finished. It was tested on Earth, in Arizona and in Hawaii.

July 26, 1971

Apollo 15 launched from Earth. David Scott and James Irwin were the astronauts.

July 30, 1971

Apollo 15 landed on the Moon, in Hadley Rille.

July 31, 1971

The astronauts pulled down the LRV, and drove it for the first time on the Moon. They drove 27.8 km in 3 days.

April 16, 1972

Apollo 16 launched from Earth. John Young and Charles Duke were the astronauts.

April 21, 1972

Apollo 16 landed on the Moon, in Descartes Highlands.

April 21-23, 1972

The astronauts drove the LRV for 27.1 km in 3 days.

December 7, 1972

Apollo 17 launched from Earth. Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt were the astronauts.

December 11, 1972

Apollo 17 landed on the Moon, in Taurus-Littrow.

December 11-13, 1972

The astronauts drove the LRV for 35.74 km in 3 days. They set the record for the fastest drive on the Moon: 18 km/h.

December 14, 1972

Apollo 17 left the Moon. The LRV was left on the Moon.

2026

There are 3 LRVs still on the Moon today. They have not moved since 1972.
